# Teaching and academic checks

Read this when authoring knowledge explanations, methods, diagnostics, and original practice. It supplements the ordered book structure in SKILL.md rather than changing it.

## Group by knowledge plus the operation being missed

Subject-wide labels such as “数学”“英语语法”“历史” are usually too broad. Prefer a teachable unit with a recognizable operation, for example:

| Area | A useful class | What the explanation must make explicit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mathematics | 分式不等式与符号分支 | 哪些值不可取、何时不等号变向、如何分类与检验边界 |
| Physics | 受力分析与系统边界 | 研究对象是谁、外力/内力如何区分、方程在什么条件下适用 |
| Chemistry | 化学计量与限量试剂 | 反应配比、物质的量换算、过量与不足如何判断 |
| Reading | 事实细节与合理推断 | 什么由原文直接陈述、什么需推理、证据是否足够 |
| Writing | 主张、论据与论证联系 | 主张是否明确、论据如何支持、反例与限定如何影响结论 |
| History | 时间顺序、材料证据与解释 | 材料能证明什么、解释是否跨越证据、是否混淆因果与先后 |
| Programming | 边界条件与循环不变量 | 初始/维持/终止条件、边界输入和代码行为如何对应 |

One question may involve several concepts. Place it once under the main failure mechanism and explain secondary knowledge before the question; do not inflate the book with duplicate copies. Split or merge classes by whether a shared method and three meaningful transfer exercises can teach the group, not by forcing an arbitrary number of classes.

## Diagnose a hypothesis, not the student

Separate internal observations from hypotheses: a visible selected answer, crossed step or handwritten comment is evidence; a presumed mental operation is an inference. Present an actionable check rather than certainty about the learner.

Useful diagnostic questions distinguish causes that would produce the same wrong answer. They should be short enough to stay with the original question and have a checkable answer. Examples of distinctions:

- Concept boundary: does the student know which objects meet the definition?
- Representation: can a sentence, diagram, passage or physical situation be translated into the appropriate relationship?
- Goal: can the student distinguish finding one value from proving uniqueness, or extracting a fact from making an inference?
- Strategy: can the student choose a method and explain its prerequisites?
- Reasoning/boundary: does a transformation remain equivalent; are exceptions, quantifiers or counterexamples handled?
- Execution: is the model correct but arithmetic, units, grammar agreement, indexing or transcription wrong?

Each answer must state what counts as correct and why. For an open diagnostic, include acceptable reasoning and how contrasting responses point to different gaps; avoid vague “自行体会” answers. A diagnosis should tell the learner what to do next when the answer is incorrect, without turning the book into an OCR/process report.

## Multiple valid angles

Choose genuinely different routes: symbolic vs graphical, direct vs reverse, example vs general argument, conservation vs force analysis, close reading vs elimination, counterexample vs full derivation, simulation vs invariant proof. State what each route establishes and what it cannot establish. A numerical illustration is not a general proof; an analogy is not a textual fact; a sample program run is not proof over all inputs.

For humanities and writing, alternatives can be different valid interpretations or evidence organizations, not fabricated certainty. A rubric should specify required claims, supporting evidence, reasoning and acceptable variants rather than requiring the model's exact wording.

## Three new questions that test transfer

Default roles, adjusted to the subject and level:

1. Apply the core idea in a clean, recognizable situation.
2. Use another representation, context or route to the same idea.
3. Test a boundary, counterexample, tempting misconception or small integration with adjacent knowledge.

Do not announce these roles in the question headings; print only “新题补练” and question numbers. Put knowledge explanations and diagnostic interpretation on the answers page. Avoid merely reproducing a published problem or changing three sets of numbers in the same template.

## Academic review

Review each new question as a solver who has not seen the intended answer. Check that all required information is present, choices do not overlap, distractors are false under the stated conditions, the claimed difficulty comes from the concept, and there is no shortcut that defeats the intended assessment. Verify dimensions, units, rounding rules, domains, equality cases, physical assumptions and logical quantifiers where relevant.

For reading/language: anchor claims in supplied material, distinguish grammar rules from stylistic preferences, and accept context-dependent variants. For history/literature: verify attributable factual claims when needed and make the interpretation standard explicit. For writing/experiments/projects: provide a reference response or method plus a fair rubric; do not label a multi-answer task as uniquely solved.

When an original problem is faulty, accurately state the mathematical/logical conflict in its solution, then teach the correct reasoning. This is substantive educational content. Statements about how OCR was performed, which repository supplied a missing option, or why a file was called a final edition belong in chat/internal records.
